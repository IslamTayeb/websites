Taking a workflow from 10 steps to 1 step (10→1) is capped at the human operator's speed. He takes breaks, slower at adopting habits, and works 9-5. Taking the person out entirely (1→0) lets you engineer the pipeline to the speed of inference down to seconds. To do that, you have to model and become the business.

Summer '25 I interned at [Soff](https://www.ycombinator.com/companies/soff), a YC startup that sold AI software to help manufacturers quote orders faster. Berni, the CEO, told me last month that Soff now makes steel. They rebranded to [Westgate Supply](https://westgatesupply.com/) and are now a real distributor.

Our customers’ sales reps spent 3 hours a day building quotes by hand: material cost, machine time, margin by order size, delivery times, etc. We eventually got good at extracting info from orders and quoting them, but deals still took days. The rep still had to review the agent's numbers, send it internally, and follow up regularly with the buyer.

That is the ceiling on the whole B2B AI pitch: we make your business' employees faster. Useful software, I helped build some of it. I’m just unconvinced there’s much money in it. There is now an AI copilot for every business out there (even [car washing](https://www.ycombinator.com/companies/nautilus)!), and techy customers build their own now. The vendor competes with Josh down the hall at DoorDash, which can afford throwing a few software engineers at making a bespoke CRM.

Investors know this too. I scraped every company [Y Combinator (YC)](http://ycombinator.com/) lists publicly and tracked industry labels by batch. B2B peaked at 69% in 2023 and has fallen about three points a year since. Industrials were 2% of the Winter 2023 batch. By Summer 2026 they were 23%. YC’s request for startups says it plainly: [“AI is moving into the physical world.”](https://www.ycombinator.com/rfs)

Both bets are the same bet. Take the human out of the loop and you stop squeezing the machine into a workflow built around people. You build the workflow around the machine. Industrials bets AI can do that work in the physical world. Full-stack bets the AI company has to own the operation to rebuild it. Westgate is both.

Nobody is retreating from AI. Spending on chips, data centers, and models is still enormous. Investors are hedging where the money comes from, because the first answer, sell more software, didn’t produce enough of it.
